*Beyond Discussion — A Practical Response to an Unequal World*

The world does not produce, consume, or manage plastic waste equally. Some countries have sophisticated recycling systems, modern waste-to-energy facilities, and regulated landfills. Others face a very different reality. Their waste management infrastructure may be limited, transportation costly, and access to appropriate treatment facilities difficult or simply unavailable. Yet plastic waste continues to accumulate everywhere. This inequality is not merely a matter of environmental awareness or policy ambition. It is also a matter of infrastructure, geography, economics, and access to practical solutions.

Global plastic production and waste generation are expected to continue growing substantially in the coming decades. Imagine a future in which 50% of plastic waste is recycled and another 20% is incinerated. Even under these optimistic assumptions, 30% would remain outside those two treatment pathways. At a hypothetical annual waste generation level of one billion tonnes, that would represent 300 million tonnes every year. This is an illustrative scenario, not a forecast of actual treatment rates. More importantly, the remaining waste would not be distributed equally across the world. In countries with established infrastructure, much of it may enter regulated landfills or other managed disposal systems. In regions with limited infrastructure, a larger share may end up in uncontrolled dumps, open burning, waterways, or the natural environment. The same global percentage can represent very different local realities.

OECD research has documented substantial differences in plastic waste management between countries and regions. These differences matter. A country with access to industrial-scale incineration and recycling infrastructure faces a fundamentally different challenge from a remote island community or rural municipality without reliable waste treatment facilities. The question is therefore not simply how much plastic the world can recycle. It is also what happens to plastic waste where conventional treatment infrastructure cannot reach, and who provides practical options for those places. Reducing plastic consumption, improving product design, expanding reuse, and increasing recycling are essential. But recycling and final treatment are not mutually exclusive responsibilities. Even successful recycling systems generate rejects and leave materials that cannot be economically or practically recovered. Those materials do not disappear because we agree that recycling should improve. They still require responsible management. The objective should not be to defend one technology against another. It should be to ensure that every part of the waste stream has an appropriate destination.

AZAD Research Group (AZADrg) is developing and openly sharing a non-commercial approach to decentralized thermal treatment of plastic waste. Our approach combines the Augean Flow Core (AFC) with Self-Sustained Oxidation Flow (SSOF), a staged gas-phase oxidation process designed for locally constructed treatment chambers. Rather than depending exclusively on large, centralized infrastructure, we are exploring how smaller units can be built, operated, maintained, and validated by local communities. Field demonstrations and independent emissions testing provide an initial technical foundation. Further validation under different operating conditions and regulatory environments remains essential. We do not claim that one technology can solve the world's plastic waste problem. Nor do we suggest that thermal treatment should replace waste prevention, reuse, or recycling. **We offer one practical treatment option for places where conventional waste management systems cannot reach.** Our principle is simple: **We do not export equipment. We share capability.** Local construction. Local operation. Local validation.

The global conversation about plastic pollution has produced valuable research, policies, commitments, and technological innovation. That conversation must continue. But millions of tonnes of plastic waste are already accumulating in places where discussions alone cannot provide treatment capacity. The world does not need another argument about whether recycling or incineration should prevail. It needs a wider range of responsible, verifiable, and locally executable solutions. Especially where the need is greatest and the available options are fewest.

This is the position of AZADrg. Not a universal answer. Not a replacement for existing systems. **One practical option in an unequal world.** Beyond Discussion.
